\section{Layers of assurance}\label{sec:assurance}

Reproducible execution and mathematical verification answer different
questions. STATADIC records five kinds of evidence separately
(\cref{tab:layers}) and treats none as a substitute for another.

\subsection{Machine-checked statements}

The Lean development builds on Mathlib with Lean \jcvalue{lean-toolchain}. A
claim registry gives each formal result a stable identifier. It records the
published source and location, the assumptions and conclusion, the relationship
to the Python implementation and the limitations. Certification compiles every
first-party module and rejects admitted proofs, custom axioms and placeholder
propositions, accepting only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}. Statement pins bind each reviewed theorem to the audited
source graph, so a changed definition invalidates its review.

The registry exports certified statements as manuscript macros, and
\cref{thm:mmd,thm:linear-mmd} are typeset from that export rather than
retyped.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{STATADIC-MMD-001}]\label{thm:mmd}
  \statadicstatement{STATADIC-MMD-001}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
  \statadicproof{STATADIC-MMD-001}
\end{proof}

Such results are deliberately narrow in scope. \Cref{thm:mmd} formalizes a
finite-moment step in the argument that the U-statistic MMD estimator is
unbiased \citep[Lemma~6]{gretton-etal-2012}. The pair moments are hypotheses.
The iid sampling construction, the reproducing-kernel interpretation,
implementation correspondence and calibration remain separate obligations.

At this revision the registry holds \jcvalue{claim-count} claims. For all
\jcvalue{claims-review-pending}, agent review was performed and human semantic
review is pending. A separate method
inventory lists \jcvalue{obligation-count} obligations across
\jcvalue{method-groups} method groups: \jcvalue{obligations-source-unresolved}
await a source and \jcvalue{obligations-open} are open. The formal programme
therefore covers selected steps, not the library's methods.

\subsection{Correspondence to implementations}

A proof about finite sums says nothing about masking with \texttt{jnp.where},
reduction order or XLA lowering. STATADIC therefore keeps a source-bound
correspondence prototype rather than asserting verification. The prototype:
\begin{itemize}
  \item records abstract-syntax-tree hashes of the \jcvalue{pinned-functions}
    Python functions that implement the MMD reductions;
  \item proves in Lean the exact rational identity behind their padding mask;
  \item lists \jcvalue{unresolved-gates} unresolved gates, including IEEE
    rounding and compiler trust.
\end{itemize}

Any change to a bound function fails the check until the change is reviewed and
the pin explicitly renewed. The current comparison reports
\jcvalue{pins-awaiting-review} changed functions awaiting pin review.

\subsection{Algebra, optimization and floating point}

For a linear kernel $k(x, y) = \langle x, y \rangle$ and weighted empirical
measures, the V-statistic MMD admits a shortcut, stated in
\cref{thm:linear-mmd} as an algorithm refinement over exact reals.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{STATADIC-MMD-002}]\label{thm:linear-mmd}
  \statadicstatement{STATADIC-MMD-002}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
  \statadicproof{STATADIC-MMD-002}
\end{proof}

The left-hand side is the V-statistic written through three Gram matrices.
STATADIC evaluates the right-hand side, centred at a reference atom, which avoids
forming those matrices (\cref{sec:performance}). Centring also avoids the
cancellation that expanding $\lVert a - b \rVert^2$ invites when coordinates
are large. The equal-mass hypothesis is essential: STATADIC's linear Gram kernel
is uncentred, so when total masses differ the reference does not cancel and
the two sides need not agree.

Human semantic review of the claim is pending. The proof does not verify
floating-point arithmetic or the JAX implementation; the numerical witness and
measured cost in \cref{sec:performance} are separate evidence.
