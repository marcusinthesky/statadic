Statistical software is used both to compute research results and to support
the claims researchers publish about them. Numerical implementations,
statistical assumptions, machine-checked proofs, validation studies and
publication outputs are nevertheless usually maintained as separate products,
which makes it hard to say which guarantees survive when a method is extended,
recombined or optimized. We present STATADIC, a Python library on JAX that
separates data representation, model specification, estimation, inference and
reporting into reusable components with explicit contracts, while retaining a
conventional fit-then-infer workflow. A companion Lean~4 development registers
machine-checked statements, links them to the Python functions they concern
and records which relationships remain unverified. Structured reports, native
PGFplots figures and restricted prediction artifacts carry computed results
into manuscripts without refitting. Every number in this paper is regenerated
from the recorded source based on revision
\texttt{\jcvalue{revision}}. STATADIC agrees with statsmodels, dcor
and lifelines to within \jcnum{agreement-aft-max} on matched fixtures. On one
retained fit, replacing HC0 by HC3 covariance raises slope-interval coverage at
$n = 25$ from \jcnum{coverage-hc0-25} to \jcnum{coverage-hc3-25} without
reaching the nominal 0.95. A linear-kernel MMD reduction avoids Gram matrices
entirely. STATADIC does not claim that its proofs verify compiled JAX programs, or
that numerical agreement establishes statistical validity; it makes these
distinctions explicit.
